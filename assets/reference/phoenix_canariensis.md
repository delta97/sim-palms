# Canary Island date palm, Phoenix canariensis

Species key: `phoenix_canariensis` (matches `palmcore.assets` species keys and `assets/library.yaml`). ASSET-001 reference sheet. All facts below cite a source in `## Sources`; `VERIFY:` marks anything not confirmed from a read page.

## Identification

Massive, solitary feather palm (pinnate fronds) with a thick trunk and a large, dense, round crown of long arching fronds. The trunk shows a diamond-shaped pattern of leaf-base scars once the bases rot off [UF-598]; the base of old trunks can carry short root initials. When the lower fronds are pruned the crown base swells into a pineapple-like knob [WP-C]. Fronds are stiff and deep green with V-shaped leaflets, the lowest of which are sharp spines [UF-598, NCSU-P]. Orange flower stalks and orange fruit [UF-598]. Very common in SoCal as large specimens on streets, in parks and at old estates.

## Look-alikes

### Canary Island date palm vs the others

Canary is the only species of the five with both a very thick trunk and feather (pinnate) fronds in a large round crown: no crownshaft [UF-21], diamond scars on a trunk 0.6-0.9 m thick, stiff fronds that stand up and then arch in a dense sphere, and spines at the frond base.

- vs the two fan palms: fan palms have palmate (hand-shaped) fronds; a pinnate (feather-shaped) frond is Canary, queen or king.
- vs queen palm: queen trunk is 0.2-0.4 m [UF-767] and smooth gray, with a looser, more drooping crown; Canary's trunk is two to three times thicker with a dense round crown of stiff fronds.
- vs king palm: king has a green crownshaft and a slim ringed trunk; Canary has no crownshaft.
- Phoenix hybrids are common and vary [UF-598]; a thinner trunk with a looser crown may be a hybrid. This project models the species only.
