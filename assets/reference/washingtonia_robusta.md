# Mexican fan palm, Washingtonia robusta

Species key: `washingtonia_robusta` (matches `palmcore.assets` species keys and `assets/library.yaml`). ASSET-001 reference sheet. All facts below cite a source in `## Sources`; `VERIFY:` marks anything not confirmed from a read page.

## Identification

Tall, slender, single-stemmed fan palm. Palmate (fan) fronds in a compact, dense, rounded head atop a trunk that tapers from a flared base. Trunk reddish-brown, weathering to light gray, ringed by closely set leaf scars [UF-827, NCSU-R]. Petioles are armed with sharp teeth and leaf bases are split and reddish-brown [UF-827]. Fronds are bright, glossy green with tips drooping [ASU]. Common names: Mexican fan palm, skyduster. Kew's POWO treats it as `Washingtonia filifera` var. `robusta` [WP-R]; this project keeps it as its own species key. It is the dominant street palm in Southern California cities; the Highland Avenue photos are good whole-tree references.

## Look-alikes

### Mexican fan palm (`washingtonia_robusta`) vs California fan palm (`washingtonia_filifera`)

Decide in this order; the first test that gives a clear answer wins. Trunk thickness alone misleads because the two hybridize and give intermediate trunks [ASU].

| Test | robusta | filifera |
|---|---|---|
| Trunk | slender: 0.2-0.3 m below the crown, flared base up to about 0.6 m [UF-827, NCSU-R, WP-R] | stout columnar: 0.3-0.6 m along its length [FEIS]; 'immensely stout', matches a Canary palm's girth [STAN] |
| Height | taller: usually 18-30 m [UF-827] | shorter: 9-15 m wild, 12-18 m cultivated [FEIS, UF-826] |
| Skirt | bare trunk above about 9 m; skirt only on young or unpruned trees [UF-827] | heavy dead-frond skirt, can run the full trunk length if untrimmed [FEIS, WP-F] |
| Crown | smaller, compact, dense, rounder; bright glossy green [ASU, WP-R] | bigger, more open crown; fronds gray-blue-green [WP-F] |
| Petioles | heavily spined, reddish-brown bases [UF-827] | smaller blunt teeth, green bases [WP-F] |
| Thread fibres | few | long white filaments between leaf segments ('cotton palm') [WP-F, STAN] |

Modeler rule: a thin-trunked palm taller than the street lamps, with a flare at the base and a small bright-green head, is robusta. A thick-trunked palm with a gray-green crown still wrapped in dead fronds is filifera. Do not widen a robusta model to make a filifera: they need separate meshes (`species_fallback` in `assets/library.yaml` maps filifera to robusta only as a stopgap).

## Color notes

- Fronds at noon (sunlit): bright, glossy, saturated green, a little yellow-green on new leaves [ASU, NCSU-R]. Sampled hex (median of green-hue pixels, approximate): `#3F5C3D` from a shaded canyon photo (#10 above); `#5F6536` from a sunlit hillside photo (#6). - Trunk: reddish-brown when young, weathering to light gray, with close, narrow ring scars [UF-827, NCSU-R].
- Dead fronds / skirt: tan to straw-brown. VERIFY: sample from photos.

## Notes for W5

Starting variants in `plans/detailed/03-palm-assets.md` W5: short 15 m (12.8-17.3), medium 21 m (17.9-24.2), tall 28 m (23.8-32.2); crown 3.5 / 4.0 / 4.5 m.

- Heights: consistent with the sources. The sources give 15-30 m (50-100 ft) and the three variants cover 12.8-32.2 m, so the documented range is covered. No change needed. The tall variant's upper cover (32.2 m) is above the 30 m maximum in [UF-827] and [WP-R], which is harmless.
- Crown diameter: the extension sources give a narrower canopy than the plan (2.4-3.0 m [UF-21, NCSU-R] vs 3.5-4.5 m). Frond reach (0.9-1.5 m blade plus 1.0-1.8 m petiole) allows 4-6 m for a full 360 degree crown, so the plan values are plausible but at the upper end for street trees, which are usually pruned thin. VERIFY against a `palms.geojson` histogram or photos before modeling; consider a narrower 'short' crown (about 3 m).
- Skirt: the tall variant should have no skirt (palms over about 9 m shed old leaves [UF-827]); the short variant may carry a short one.
- Other species, for completeness: filifera 12 / 16 m fits 9-15 m (wild) and 12-18 m (cultivated), but its plan crown (4.5 / 5.5 m) is above the 3.0-4.6 m in [UF-826]; Canary 10 / 14 / 18 m fits 10-20 m and crown 8 / 10 / 11 m is inside 6-12 m; queen 9 / 13 m fits 8-15 m and crown 5 / 6 m is inside 5-7.6 m; king 10 / 14 m fits the cultivated 9-15 m and crown 4.5 / 5.5 m is inside 3.7-8 m. No variant was found to be outside its documented range; the only mismatches are the two crown sizes noted.
